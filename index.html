Understanding the supply side

Designing the AI as a translator, not a judge

Building buyer trust also meant understanding the artisans behind each piece. Conversations with makers in Jaipur, Thrissur, and Chandni Chowk revealed that they had their own concerns about how AI might represent their work.

Design Guardrails

These conversations shaped three lasting guardrails. They may not appear directly on the screen, but they guide how the AI compares, explains, and represents every piece.

01

Design Principle

Price the craft,
not just the material

Making charges are explained through the work behind the piece, such as “35 hours of Kumartuli embossing,” so buyers understand the value of the craft.Making charges are explained through the work, like “35 hours of Kumartuli embossing.”

02

Design Principle

Verified, not ranked

Artisans are either platform-verified or not listed. There are no star ratings or algorithmic rankings that reduce their work to a score.Artisans are verified or not listed. No star ratings or rankings.

03

Design Principle

Artisans are partners,
not sellers

The artisan’s name and origin appear alongside the product title, making every listing feel like a partnership rather than a commodity.The artisan’s name and origin sit beside every product title.

System Guardrails: What Kreston AI Will Never Do

01

Guardrail

No resale predictions

AI shows the piece’s current verified value without making speculative forecasts.AI shows today’s verified value, never a forecast.

02

Guardrail

No biased comparisons

AI compares verified details such as price, technique, weight, and certification. It never ranks artisans or pushes one piece over another.AI compares verified facts. It never ranks artisans or pushes a piece.

03

Guardrail

No guessing when data is missing

When authentication data is incomplete, AI clearly flags the gap and brings in a human expert instead of filling it with assumptions.If data is incomplete, AI flags the gap and brings in an expert.

My learnings

What this project taught me about designing for trust

Reflections from designing a high-value jewellery e-commerce experience where every pixel carried the weight of credibility.

01

No single element builds trust alone. It's the consistent layering across every touchpoint.

The lesson

“Design trust as an ecosystem, not a checklist.”

02

Users accepted AI only when a human expert was one tap away.

The lesson

“Always pair automated intelligence with visible human accountability.”

03

Early prototypes packed every trust signal on screen. Users felt overwhelmed.

The lesson

“Progressive disclosure beats information overload.”

04

Buyers need to feel the piece is meant for them. Storytelling outperformed data.

The lesson

“Pair rational proof with emotional narrative.”
