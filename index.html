Overview

Bridging the Gap Between Luxury Jewellery and Online Trust

A 4-week design sprint exploring how a digital-only jewellery platform can make gold, silver, platinum, and diamond purchases online feel as safe as walking into a store.

AI-assisted trust signals surfaced early in the funnel

Certification and pricing transparency made high price points feel manageable

About the project

High-value purchases need more than trust badges

What is Kreston

Where India's artisan jewellery
finally finds its buyer.

Connecting urban Indian buyers with independent artisans - handcrafted gold, diamond, and silver pieces rarely found in mainstream retail.

The opportunity

1000+ artisans.
Zero national reach.

Giving buyers access to one-of-a-kind pieces with a story behind every design, moving locally trusted talent into a national spotlight.

The trust challenge

Who made this? Is it real?
Can I trust the price?

Because Kreston works with independents, customers question purity and certification. That anxiety is the core design problem.

Gaps that are still uncovered

Where trust breaks down

Certifications buyers can't read

Certified. Yet Still confusing.

BIS, GIA, and hallmark badges shown - but buyers have no idea what any of them mean.

No way to verify the artisan

No name. No story. No proof.

Every piece is handcrafted - but the maker is invisible. No profile, no background, no trust signal.

Pricing feels arbitrary

A price. No breakdown. No reason.

A ₹24,000 pendant with no breakdown. Buyers can't tell if it's fair - or just inflated.

Metal purity is unverifiable

Claimed, never proven

Purity is claimed, not proven. Buyers accept the label without any way to verify it independently.

Returns feel risky on high-value items

Confidence ends at checkout

Returning a ₹50,000 piece feels uncertain. Policies exist - but confidence in the process doesn't.

Post-purchase support is absent

Support stops at the receipt

After checkout, buyers are on their own. No care guide, no resizing help, no expert follow-up - the relationship ends the moment payment clears.

The AI trust layer

Choosing between Chatbot VS Inline AI

The obvious solution was a chatbot. Every competitor had one. But the more I stress-tested it against my research, the more it felt like a category error.

The Chatbot

Feels like a biased assistant

  • Requires the user to formulate a question first
  • Feels like a sales tool trying to close the deal
  • Reactive — answers only what's asked
  • Unpredictable live responses, hard to audit

Chatbot Approach

Feels like a salesperson. Every competitor has one. Doesn't solve the real problem.

Inline AI Content

Feels like objective infrastructure

  • Answers anxiety before it's formulated as a question
  • Feels native to the app's architecture, not persuasive
  • Pre-rendered, auditable, and reviewable
  • Grounded in verified seller data — not general knowledge

Inline AI Approach

AI works quietly in the background. Adds helpful info directly into the experience.

Where the chatbot still fits

The chatbot isn't gone—it appears at four moments where users still need to ask: the PDP, certification detail, payment confirmation, and order tracking.

Understanding the supply side

Designing the AI as a translator, not a judge

Marketplace design is a two-sided coin. Building buyer trust meant understanding the supply side too — research with artisans in Jaipur, Thrissur, and Chandni Chowk revealed anxieties as real as the buyers'.

Design Guardrails

These conversations shaped three permanent design guardrails - decisions that don't appear on any screen, but govern everything that does.

01

Design Principle

Pricing the art,
not just the materials

Making charge isn't a hidden fee — it's the craft story. AI uses Maker Lineage data to explain: "35 hours of Kumartuli embossing." We price the art, not just materials.

02

Design Principle

Verified by craft,
not ranked by algorithm

No star ratings or algorithmic ranking. Sellers are either "Platform Verified" or not listed — protecting artisan reputation from algorithm games.

03

Design Principle

Artisans as co-brands,
not sellers

Artisan name and origin get top billing alongside the product title — every page feels like a co-branded partnership, not a commodity listing.

System Guardrails: What Kreston AI Will Never Do

01

Guardrail

No resale predictions

Indian buyers see gold as investment, but forecasting creates false confidence. The AI shows current verified value — never speculative predictions.

02

Guardrail

No competitor comparisons

Kreston's AI is a translator, not a salesperson. Comparing sellers or brands introduces bias and undermines the neutrality that makes the trust layer credible.

03

Guardrail

No guessing under data uncertainty

If core authentication data is missing, the system flags it and escalates to a human expert. It never fills gaps with generalization. Uncertainty is surfaced, never papered over.

What's next

If Kreston were a real product, here's the next design problem

The buyer-facing trust layer was the design thesis of this project. But a two-sided marketplace has two sets of anxieties - and the seller side is the design problem this prototype intentionally deferred.

Next priority

Seller Onboarding UX

Make certification upload frictionless — and clearly flag which data gaps trigger low confidence — so buyer trust is built upstream.

Core Tension

How to make rigorous authentication feel empowering, not bureaucratic?

Next priority

Proof Vault Sharing UX

Turning the Proof Vault into a shareable handoff — so certificates and provenance records pass to family with the weight of a physical document, not a screenshot.

Core Tension

“How to design a share moment that feels like handing over a document?”

Future surface

Regional Language Support

Buyers in Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and West Bengal don't all transact in English. AI cert explanations in their first language isn't localization — it's a trust multiplier.

Core Tension

How to maintain precision of technical certification across translations?

My learnings

What this project taught me about designing for trust

Reflections from designing a high-value jewellery e-commerce experience where every pixel carried the weight of credibility.

01

Trust Is a System, Not a Feature

No single element builds trust alone. It's the consistent layering across every touchpoint.

The lesson

“Design trust as an ecosystem, not a checklist.”

02

AI Needs a Human Safety Net

Users accepted AI only when a human expert was one tap away.

The lesson

“Always pair automated intelligence with visible human accountability.”

03

Complexity ≠ Comprehensiveness

Early prototypes packed every trust signal on screen. Users felt overwhelmed.

The lesson

“Progressive disclosure beats information overload.”

04

Emotion Drives High-Value Decisions

Buyers need to feel the piece is meant for them. Storytelling outperformed data.

The lesson

“Pair rational proof with emotional narrative.”
